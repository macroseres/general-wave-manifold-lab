02 — Rarefaction and the local chart at infinity

The global view does not contain every projective direction as an ordinary finite point. When the rarefaction field has a singularity at projective infinity, the application provides a local reciprocal chart (eta,Z).

For the validated regression preset, the point at Z=0 is a saddle. Its four local half-separatrices are represented in the reciprocal chart. Two stable half-branches lie on Z=0; they must not be forced into the finite z chart. The unstable branches enter opposite signs of Z and can transition to the finite chart where Z != 0.

Interpretation rule

A curve absent from the finite global chart is not automatically absent mathematically. Use the local chart to inspect branches that live on the projective boundary.

Current controls

The stable and unstable separatrix visibility controls also determine which corresponding branches are shown in the local chart.

Flow orientation and branch labels

The local chart labels the four half-separatrices as Ws-, Ws+, Wu-, and Wu+. The superscript notation in the interface distinguishes stable and unstable invariant directions; the +/- suffix distinguishes the two local half-branches and is not an eigenvalue sign.

Arrowheads show the orientation of the rarefaction vector field in physical integration time. Thus the arrows on stable branches point toward the saddle, while the arrows on unstable branches point away from it. The dashed line Z=0 is the projective boundary, not an ordinary finite-z axis.
