\section{Composite waves: canonical construction boundary}

This chapter opens the composite-wave block.  It fixes the mathematical
construction before any numerical surface meshing or Three.js rendering is
introduced.

Let $\Gamma\subset\mathcal W$ be a moving curve.  Its local endpoint states
are $U_-(P)=\pi_-(P)$ and $U_+(P)=\pi_+(P)$.  The two oriented saturations are
\[
 \operatorname{sat}_{\mathrm{forw}}(\Gamma)
 =\bigcup_{P\in\Gamma}\pi_-^{-1}\!\left(U_-(P)\right),
 \qquad
 \operatorname{sat}_{\mathrm{back}}(\Gamma)
 =\bigcup_{P\in\Gamma}\pi_+^{-1}\!\left(U_+(P)\right).
\]
Thus forward saturation fixes the moving left state on each leaf, whereas
backward saturation fixes the moving right state.

For the rarefaction set $\mathcal R$ used as seed, the canonical composite
constructions are
\[
 \boxed{\mathcal K_-=
 \operatorname{sat}_{\mathrm{forw}}(\mathcal R)\cap\mathcal S^-},
 \qquad
 \boxed{\mathcal K_+=
 \operatorname{sat}_{\mathrm{back}}(\mathcal R)\cap\mathcal S^+}.
\]
Here $\mathcal S^-$ and $\mathcal S^+$ mean the corresponding non-trivial
sonic sheets.  In the finite $z$ chart their implicit factors are
\[
 \Phi_-(\tau,Y,z)=Y\det M-2K,
 \qquad
 \Phi_+(\tau,Y,z)=Y\det M+2K.
\]
Consequently a candidate point of $\mathcal K_-$ must belong simultaneously
to a forward saturation leaf and satisfy $\Phi_-=0$; a candidate point of
$\mathcal K_+$ must belong to a backward saturation leaf and satisfy
$\Phi_+=0$.

This definition does \emph{not} yet prescribe a numerical algorithm for
constructing the two-dimensional saturated set, continuing its intersection
with a sonic sheet, selecting connected branches, or imposing an additional
admissibility criterion.  Those are subsequent stages of the scientific
pipeline.

\paragraph{Current numerical implementation.}
The preceding paragraph describes the scope of this definition, not the absence
of numerical implementations. Sampling and branch tracing now exist in
\texttt{src/math/state/compositeTrace.js}. They provide candidates rather than
a proof of global coverage. In particular, its reciprocal search residual uses
$Z^2\Phi_\pm=Z\widehat\Phi_\pm$ on the overlap. The additional factor can make
the residual small near $Z=0$ without satisfying the canonical reciprocal sonic
condition. Boundary candidates require direct reciprocal verification; the
regularized residual alone is not a sonic certificate.
